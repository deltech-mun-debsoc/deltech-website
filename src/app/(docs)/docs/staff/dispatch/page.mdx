`/admin/blog` is the review queue for the Dispatch. Nothing published itself.

<Screenshot src="/docs/blog-moderation.webp" alt="The Dispatch review queue showing articles needing review" caption="Needs review at the top with a count badge, recently reviewed below." />

## The queue

Two lists. **Needs review** carries everything waiting on you: newly submitted articles
and ones you previously sent back that have been resubmitted. It has a count badge, so
you can see the backlog without opening the page.

**Recently reviewed** shows the last thirty days, so you can find something you handled
last week.

## Reviewing

Open an article and you get the full rendered preview exactly as a reader would see it,
plus a sidebar with the author, when it was submitted, its reading time, its tags and
any previous review note.

Three decisions:

| Decision | Effect | Email sent |
|---|---|---|
| **Approve** | Published immediately at `/blog`. | Article approved |
| **Request changes** | Back to the author with your note. | Changes requested |
| **Reject** | Not published, with your note. | Article not published |

## Write a useful note

For both **request changes** and **reject**, your note is shown to the author inline on
their own page and included in their email. It is the only feedback they get. "Needs
work" wastes a round trip; "the second and third sections repeat each other, and the
quote in paragraph four needs a source" gets a fixed article back.

## Requesting changes versus rejecting

Request changes when you expect a better version. Reject when the piece is not right for
the Dispatch at all. Rejection is not deletion: the article still exists, and the author
can be told a different angle would be welcome.

## Deleting

Admin only. Rarely necessary, since rejecting already keeps a piece off the public site.

## Checking after publishing

Look at `/blog` and at the article's own page after approving. The Dispatch index shows
the newest post prominently, so a bad cover image or a missing subtitle is immediately
visible to everyone.
