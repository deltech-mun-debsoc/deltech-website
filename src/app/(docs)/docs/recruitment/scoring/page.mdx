Scoring is per criterion, weighted, and reported on a single comparable scale.

## Submitting an evaluation

You give a score for **every criterion** in the rubric, written remarks of up to four
thousand characters, and one **recommendation**: select, hold or reject.

The form rejects an evaluation that has an unknown criterion, is missing a criterion, or
has a score outside a criterion's range. You cannot half-submit.

## How the number is produced

<Steps>
1. Each criterion's score is **normalised to a 0 to 1 range** against its own maximum.

2. Each normalised score is multiplied by its **weight**.

3. The weighted values are combined and reported on a **0 to 10 scale**.
</Steps>

The normalisation step is the one worth understanding. It means a criterion scored out of
5 and one scored out of 100 contribute according to their **weights**, not according to
their maxima. Without it, any criterion with a bigger scale would quietly dominate.

## Recommendations and the panel verdict

Each evaluator recommends independently. The panel's verdict is resolved from all of them:

- a **clear majority** wins;
- **any tie becomes HOLD**.

A tie never resolves in either direction by accident. If the panel is split, the candidate
is held for a human to look at.

## Revising an evaluation

Evaluations are **append-only**. Revising does not overwrite the previous version; it adds
a new one that supersedes it. The whole chain stays visible in the dossier.

Voiding an evaluation is Administrative Council only.

## Who can see whose scores

By default you see **your own** evaluations. Seeing other evaluators' scores is a separate
capability, held by Senior and Administrative Council.

This is deliberate: it stops evaluators anchoring on each other's numbers before they have
formed their own view.

## Writing scores that are worth having

- **Use the whole scale.** A rubric where everyone scores 7 to 9 carries no information.
- **Score the criterion, not the candidate.** Someone can be excellent on content and weak
  on courtesy; that is what a rubric is for.
- **The remarks matter more than the number.** At finalisation, the Administrative Council
  reads remarks. A score of 6.4 with no remarks tells them nothing they can act on.
- **Submit before you leave the room.** Written an hour later, remarks are worse.
