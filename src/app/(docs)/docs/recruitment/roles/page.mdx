Three council roles, assigned **per cycle**. Someone can be Junior Council on this cycle
and nothing on the next.

## What each can do

### Junior Council

The working level. A JC can:

- create and edit group discussion panels;
- assign candidates and staff to them;
- run a GD session: start, pause, resume, finish, abort;
- mark attendance;
- advance and hold candidates;
- preview and apply imports;
- read the audit trail.

A JC **can only score where a Senior Council member ticked "may score"** on their
assignment to that group. Without the tick they can assist a session without evaluating
it.

A JC cannot touch the interview stage at all. Visiting it redirects them.

### Senior Council

Everything above, plus:

- the entire interview surface;
- bypassing a candidate past the group discussion;
- withdrawing a candidate;
- disqualifying a candidate.

### Administrative Council

Everything above, plus the powers that change the shape of the cycle:

- creating, configuring and transitioning the cycle;
- reopening a finished session;
- voiding an evaluation;
- reversing a bypass;
- reconsidering and overriding a candidate;
- finalising results;
- adding a candidate to the society;
- configuring imports.

## Two gates, not one

Every action is checked twice: **does this role have the capability**, and **does the
cycle's current state allow it**. Both must pass.

So an Administrative Council member cannot score a candidate in a cycle that is still in
draft, and nobody can start a session in a cancelled one. If an action you expect to have
is missing, check the cycle's state before assuming it is a permissions problem. See
[the cycle lifecycle](/docs/recruitment/lifecycle).

## How you get a role

Two ways.

**Derived from your admin role.** An admin becomes Administrative Council, a maintainer
becomes Senior Council, a sub-maintainer becomes Junior Council.

**Assigned explicitly** on the cycle, which overrides the derived role in either
direction: it can promote someone with no admin access at all, and it can demote someone
who has one.

A **global admin always outranks the cycle**, so an Administrative Council seat can never
be taken away from them by a cycle assignment. That is the escape hatch for a
misconfigured cycle.

When authority came from an admin role rather than an explicit assignment, the audit
entry records that fact.

## The sub-maintainer role

An account whose only surface is recruitment. It lands on `/recruitment` and is
structurally locked out of `/admin`. Use it for council members who should never see
delegate data or payments.
