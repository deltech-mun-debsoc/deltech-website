`/recruitment/candidates/<id>` is everything recorded about one person, on one page. Read
it before an interview and before a decision.

<Screenshot src="/docs/dossier.webp" alt="A candidate dossier showing scores, remarks and stage history" caption="Scores from every evaluator, the verbatim application response, and the full stage history." />

## What is on it

| Section | Contents |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | Name, contact details, and whatever the application form captured. |
| **Group discussion** | The session, per-evaluator scores and remarks, and the aggregate. |
| **Interview** | The session, scores, remarks and recommendations. |
| **Application response** | Their form answers, **verbatim**, as submitted. |
| **Provenance** | Which import this candidate came from and when. |
| **Stage history** | Every state they have been in and every transition, with who and when. |

## When there is no group discussion record

The page says which of three things happened, rather than showing a blank:

- **bypassed**, with who authorised it, when, and the reason they gave;
- **not required**, per the cycle's stage rules;
- **pending**, meaning it has not happened yet.

Making the distinction explicit means nobody has to guess whether a missing GD is a
decision or an oversight.

## Whose scores you see

Your own by default. Senior and Administrative Council see everyone's. See
[scoring and the rubric](/docs/recruitment/scoring).

## The candidate list

`/recruitment/candidates` filters by stage and result, and searches by name or email. The
search can optionally look **inside stored form answers**, which is how you find "everyone
who mentioned debating" without reading every dossier.

From the list you can export all candidates, export a selection, or email a selection.

## Reading one properly

The verbatim application response is the most under-used part of the page. It is what the
candidate actually wrote, before anyone summarised it. Read it before you read the scores,
so the scores do not colour it.
