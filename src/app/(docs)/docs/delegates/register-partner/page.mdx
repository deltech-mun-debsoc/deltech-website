Step 3 changes depending on what you picked in step 2. This is the only step in the
form that does that.

## If your first choice is a double delegation committee

The form asks for your partner instead of a second preference, and the partner is
**required**. You cannot skip this step or continue without one.

| Field | Required |
|---|---|
| Partner's full name | Yes |
| Partner's email | Yes |
| Partner's phone | Yes |
| Partner's institution | No |
| Partner's MUN experience | No |

Your partner receives their own email telling them you have registered them, so use
their real address and tell them to expect it.

<Callout type="warning">
Your partner does **not** fill in their own registration form. You register the pair.
If they also register separately you end up with two records and the secretariat has
to untangle it.
</Callout>

### Why this is enforced

A double delegation seat is held by exactly two people. Allowing one person to
register alone would create a half-filled seat that someone has to chase. The rule is
checked again on the server when you submit, so it cannot be worked around.

## If your first choice is a single delegation committee

The form asks for a **second preference** instead: another committee and another
portfolio. Both are optional, and you can leave the step empty.

Your first-choice committee is removed from this list, since a second preference for
the same committee would not tell the secretariat anything new.

<Callout type="tip">
Fill it in. Popular committees run out, and a delegate with no second preference gets
allotted whatever is left rather than the next thing they actually wanted.
</Callout>
